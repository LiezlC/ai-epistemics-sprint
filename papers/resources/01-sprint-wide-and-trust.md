## Sprint-wide

### 1. State of the Field: AI for Epistemics and Coordination

[LessWrong report](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/uw8RQ8yjKexnhTw6E/state-of-the-field-ai-for-epistemics-and-coordination), Ben N and Alex Csaky, 2026. **Read:** full report in browser, including methods and trust/provenance sections. **Access:** HTML only; no public PDF located. **Summary:** maps the field and reports that adoption, pilot partners, workflow fit, trust, and procurement often constrain impact more than prototype construction. Its recommendations include deployment testing, maintained evaluations, field-building, and usable epistemic infrastructure. Its trust section stresses that provenance does not establish truth, reliability scoring can encode contested judgments, and infrastructure matters only when consumers use it. **Evidence and limits:** the report synthesizes about 65 in-depth field-strategy interviews, informed by 90-plus wider conversations. Funders are all EA/AI-safety-adjacent; interview coverage of verification/provenance and persuasion is thin and relies more on desk research; the in-depth sample skews to builders and funders. Treat deployment bottlenecks as an informed field assessment, not a representative survey. **Project angle:** directly supports a small test of whether provenance signals survive a multi-agent workflow and whether a downstream agent uses them. Keep evidence independence and human auditability measurable.

### 2. “What’s Important in ‘AI for Epistemics’?”

[Forethought essay](https://www.forethought.org/research/whats-important-in-ai-for-epistemics), Lukas Finnveden, 2024. **Read:** full web article. **Local:** [PDF](../whats-important-in-ai-for-epistemics.pdf) · [text](../text/whats-important-in-ai-for-epistemics.txt) · [Forethought download](https://www.forethought.org/api/article/whats-important-in-ai-for-epistemics.pdf). **Summary:** argues that early epistemic tools may shape path-dependent norms and capabilities, and offers heuristics for prioritizing interventions. It connects near-term decision support to longer-term epistemic outcomes. **Evidence and limits:** strategic argument, not an impact evaluation; conclusions depend on assumptions about AI progress, path dependence, and institutional uptake. **Project angle:** test a narrow infrastructure claim in the smallest tractable setting, then identify which adoption step a result would support.

### 3. “AI Impacts on Epistemics: The Good, the Bad and the Ugly”

[Forethought essay](https://www.forethought.org/research/ai-impacts-on-epistemics-the-good-the-bad-and-the-ugly), Owen Cotton-Barratt and Rose Hadshar, 2026. **Read:** full web article. **Local:** [PDF](../ai-impacts-on-epistemics-the-good-the-bad-and-the-ugly.pdf) · [text](../text/ai-impacts-on-epistemics-the-good-the-bad-and-the-ugly.txt) · [Forethought download](https://www.forethought.org/api/article/ai-impacts-on-epistemics-the-good-the-bad-and-the-ugly.pdf). **Summary:** organizes potential effects into beneficial epistemic support, unintended degradation, and deliberate manipulation. It highlights feedback loops that may amplify either direction. **Evidence and limits:** scenario taxonomy and argument, not empirical measurement; coverage is selective and the trajectory claims are uncertain. **Project angle:** include an adversarial condition where repeated agent relays inflate apparent support, alongside a clean provenance condition.

### 4. “Design Sketches for a More Sensible World”

[Forethought article series](https://www.forethought.org/research/design-sketches-for-a-more-sensible-world), Owen Cotton-Barratt, Lizka Vaintrob, Oly Sourbut, and Rose Hadshar, 2026. **Read:** full article series landing page and linked sections, including collective epistemics and defense-favored coordination. **Local:** [PDF](../design-sketches-for-a-more-sensible-world.pdf) · [text](../text/design-sketches-for-a-more-sensible-world.txt) · [Forethought download](https://www.forethought.org/api/article/design-sketches-for-a-more-sensible-world.pdf). **Summary:** presents buildable concepts for collective epistemics, decision support, strategic awareness, and coordination that favors defense. **Evidence and limits:** design sketches with feasibility judgments, not tested products or measured effects. **Project angle:** a tamper-evident action recorder is a small infrastructure component that could help realize the auditable coordination sketches. Scope it to verifiable provenance of evidence and handoffs, rather than claiming a full trust layer.

### 5. “Project Ideas: Epistemics”

[Lukas Finnveden’s post](https://lukasfinnveden.substack.com/p/project-ideas-epistemics), 2024, updated 2025 per the sprint page. **Read:** full web post. **Access:** HTML; no public PDF found. **Summary:** motivates epistemic interventions and collects concrete research and product directions, including ways AI might improve learning and agreement or increase persuasion toward false beliefs. **Evidence and limits:** agenda-setting essay; proposals are not comparative evaluations. **Project angle:** use its project-menu style to describe a minimal paired experiment, including who would consume the trust signal and which failure would block adoption.

### 6. “The Most Interesting Startup Idea I’ve Seen Recently: AI for Epistemics”

[80,000 Hours article](https://80000hours.org/2024/05/project-idea-ai-for-epistemics/), Benjamin Todd, 2024. **Read:** full article. **Access:** HTML; no public PDF found. **Summary:** frames decision support and forecasting as a potentially high-leverage venture, with near-term commercial applications that could create capabilities and a later public-interest use case. **Evidence and limits:** short career and startup argument, illustrated partly with platform performance; not a controlled impact study. **Project angle:** name the initial consumer and integration point. An append-only provenance viewer could target agent-framework developers or auditors rather than a vague general audience.

### 7. “A Full Epistemic Stack: Knowledge Commons for the 21st Century”

[LessWrong post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/DMswzhPQqkqx2XAma/a-full-epistemic-stack-knowledge-commons-for-the-21st-1), Oliver Sourbut and Ben Goldhaber, 2025. **Read:** full post. **Access:** HTML; no public PDF found. **Summary:** sketches an integrated knowledge infrastructure spanning individual, institutional, and social belief formation. The post treats projects as pieces in a larger stack. **Evidence and limits:** conceptual architecture, not an implemented reference system or empirical result. **Project angle:** specify where signed action traces sit relative to evidence retrieval, source reliability, and human review; avoid treating provenance as a verdict about truth.

### 8. Multi-Agent Risks from Advanced AI

[Cooperative AI Foundation technical report / arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14143), Hammond et al., 2025. **Read:** full 96-page report PDF and extracted text. **Local:** [PDF](../multi-agent-risks-from-advanced-ai.pdf) · [text](../text/multi-agent-risks-from-advanced-ai.txt). **Summary:** taxonomizes multi-agent failure as miscoordination, conflict, and collusion, with risk factors including information asymmetries, network effects, selection pressures, destabilizing dynamics, commitment problems, emergent agency, and multi-agent security. It synthesizes conceptual analysis, real-world cases, and experimental evidence. **Evidence and limits:** broad technical report and taxonomy rather than a single controlled test; the inclusion of real-world analogues does not establish prevalence or inevitability in deployed AI systems. **Project angle:** directly relevant to repeated handoffs: a chain of agents can manufacture apparent corroboration by relaying one originating observation. Preserve source lineage and test whether the final agent can distinguish copied evidence from independent observations.

## Track 2: Trust Infrastructure

### 19. C2PA Technical Specification v2.4

[Official C2PA v2.4 specification](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/index.html), especially [Content Credentials technical specification](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/specs/C2PA_Specification.html). **Read:** full 309-page specification PDF and extracted text. **Local:** [PDF](../c2pa-content-credentials-specification-v2.4.pdf) · [text](../text/c2pa-content-credentials-specification-v2.4.txt). The page’s Download menu lists older released PDFs, but the v2.4 attachment URL resolves and validates as a PDF. **Summary:** specifies signed manifests containing assertions and content bindings to record asset provenance and transformations. Validation checks signatures, credentials, bindings, and assertions; the standard explicitly avoids deciding whether the assertions are true or good. **Limits:** content credentials are opt-in and depend on ecosystem adoption, credential trust, retention, and interface design. A valid manifest establishes integrity and signer-related provenance within the trust model, not truth, completeness, or independence. **Project angle:** use a signed provenance envelope for each observation and agent handoff; test if readers can detect when multiple summaries derive from one source. **Related tool:** [c2patool](https://github.com/contentauth/c2patool), the C2PA command-line implementation, is useful for inspecting or validating content credentials.

### 20. Community Notes open dataset

[Official Community Notes repository and data documentation](https://github.com/twitter/communitynotes). **Read:** repository documentation and released data descriptions. **Access:** dataset remains online; not copied locally. **Summary:** X publishes note, rating, and status data for its community-based context system; its bridging-based ranking aims to surface notes rated helpful by contributors with differing prior rating patterns. **Limits:** public release omits deleted posts and related records; visible data are platform-governed and do not expose all exposure, impression, or downstream belief outcomes. Account and note activity are not independent truth labels. **Project angle:** use as an example of open, inspectable provenance and disagreement data, while keeping source diversity separate from correctness. Do not treat multiple raters or agent handoffs as independent evidence by default.

### 21. “From Birdwatch to Community Notes, from Twitter to X: Four Years of Community-Based Content Moderation”

[arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09585), Mohammadi et al., v4, 2026. **Read:** full 47-page PDF and extracted text. **Local:** [PDF](../from-birdwatch-to-community-notes.pdf) · [text](../text/from-birdwatch-to-community-notes.txt). **Method and findings:** descriptive analysis of four years of Notes and ratings; authors parse language across Notes, extract URLs from English-language Notes, identify topics, and build monthly contributor interaction networks. They release a curated dataset and code and review prior work. The paper reports highly skewed note and rating activity, with a small contributor share responsible for much of the work. It is descriptive, not a causal estimate of whether Community Notes changes beliefs or platform outcomes. **Limits:** the authors downloaded public files on April 7, 2025 and note that deleted posts, notes, and ratings are absent; analysis relies on available releases and platform access. **Project angle:** interaction networks can help distinguish repeated pathways from independent contributors. Adapt that idea to agent handoff graphs: measure unique root observations, not just the number of agents repeating a claim.

### 22. “AI for AI for Epistemics”

[Forethought essay](https://www.forethought.org/research/ai-for-ai-for-epistemics), 2026. **Read:** full web article. **Local:** [PDF](../ai-for-ai-for-epistemics.pdf) · [text](../text/ai-for-ai-for-epistemics.txt) · [Forethought download](https://www.forethought.org/api/article/ai-for-ai-for-epistemics.pdf). **Summary:** asks how AI capabilities may accelerate epistemic research and development, then maps risks including epistemic misalignment and trust lock-in. It recommends anticipating data needs and supporting open, auditable infrastructure. **Evidence and limits:** strategic analysis, not an empirical demonstration that auditability changes model behavior. **Project angle:** frame provenance as defense against lock-in: a record needs stable source identity, derivation lineage, and tamper evidence, while a separate evaluation tests whether agents use those signals accurately.

## Track 2 example-only links

### 38. OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions

[OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/semconv/registry/attributes/gen-ai/). **Read:** current official registry page. **Access:** living specification; no separate PDF expected. **Summary:** standardizes names and structures for model, agent, tool, and related trace attributes. The current GenAI conventions are marked in development, so names and schemas can change. **Limits:** instrumentation supports observability; it does not itself provide cryptographic signatures, append-only guarantees, identity assurance, or resistance to log-owner tampering. Sensitive prompt/response content also requires explicit capture choices. **Project angle:** instrument the handoff experiment with OTel spans, then layer signed event hashes and externally witnessed checkpoints over the trace. Keep the schema version explicit.

### 39. Certificate Transparency, RFC 6962

[IETF RFC 6962](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc6962). **Read:** full RFC HTML and local PDF with extracted text. **Local:** [PDF](../rfc-6962-certificate-transparency.pdf) · [text](../text/rfc-6962-certificate-transparency.txt). **Correction:** the sprint links RFC 6962, issued as an experimental protocol in 2013. It has since been obsoleted by [RFC 9162, Certificate Transparency Version 2.0](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc9162), issued in 2021. RFC 9162 is useful current context, though the local archived copy follows the sprint’s actual link. **Summary:** CT uses append-only Merkle logs, signed tree heads, inclusion proofs, and consistency proofs so observers can check that entries appear and the log grows consistently. **Limits:** append-only proofs do not by themselves prevent a log from showing split views; monitors and cross-observer gossip/witnessing matter. This is a certificate transparency design, not a ready-made agent log. **Project angle:** borrow the transparency pattern: hash each signed handoff event into a Merkle log, issue checkpoints, and have an independent verifier compare consistency and root-observation identity.

### 40. AVeriTeC: A Dataset for Real-World Claim Verification with Evidence from the Web

[NeurIPS 2023 Datasets & Benchmarks paper / arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13117), Schlichtkrull, Guo, and Vlachos. **Read:** full 40-page PDF and extracted text. **Local:** [PDF](../averitec-dataset-real-world-claim-verification.pdf) · [text](../text/averitec-dataset-real-world-claim-verification.txt). **Method and findings:** introduces 4,568 real-world claims from 50 fact-checking organizations with evidence-backed question-answer pairs and written justifications. Multi-round annotation addresses context dependence, insufficient evidence, and temporal leakage; verdict agreement is κ = 0.619. A baseline evaluates multistep open-web verification. In a 20-item retrieval analysis, Google queries generated from claims and model questions found useful evidence in 9 cases, compared with 16 using gold questions, exposing query generation as a bottleneck. **Limits:** claim mix is shaped by fact-checking coverage and is imbalanced toward refuted claims; evidence retrieval and labels remain bounded by available web sources and annotator judgments. Benchmark performance does not establish reliability on arbitrary claims or changing live sources. **Project angle:** adapt claim-level evidence records and time-aware evidence capture for a handoff audit. Score whether the final answer is supported by the cited root sources and whether ostensibly separate citations trace back to the same observation.

## Candidate study: handoffs that impersonate corroboration

This lane has adjacent work on trust signals, reliability scoring, community notes, audit logs, and multi-agent collusion. The sharper question is whether repeated handoffs create *false independence*: can a model or human auditor mistake one observation echoed through a chain for several independent observations? This is adjacent to, but narrower than, general provenance or agent-coordination work. The closest known overlap is in [PROJECT-ANGLES.md](../../PROJECT-ANGLES.md), especially Bara’s “Epistemic Sybil Resistance” preprint; compare its threat model and proposed defenses before claiming novelty.

A sprint-sized paired design: create matched claims supported by either (A) the same underlying observation copied through several agent handoffs, or (B) genuinely independent observations. Keep wording, number of agents, and surface evidence count matched. Give the final agent the same provenance UI in both conditions, then test whether it calibrates confidence, cites root sources, and flags dependence correctly. Add one adversarial case where an agent relabels a copied source as independent. Report task-level results and disagreements, not just aggregate accuracy. This measures signal use in a controlled synthetic setup; it does not establish real-world prevalence or broad model performance.
